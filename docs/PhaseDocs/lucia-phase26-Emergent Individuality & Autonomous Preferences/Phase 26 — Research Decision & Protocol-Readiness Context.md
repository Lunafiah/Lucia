# 5. P26 Scientific Target

## Path-Dependent Behavioral Differentiation — PDBD

PDBD is the primary candidate system-level phenomenon.

However, simple history dependence is insufficient.

### C1 — History Dependence

Behavior changes as accumulated history changes.

This is expected in stateful, adaptive or learning systems and is therefore **not sufficient as a P26 result**.

### C2 — Matched-Exposure Differentiation

Replicas exposed to statistically matched environments can nevertheless develop persistent behavioral differences.

This is the first non-trivial P26 target.

It prevents the trivial explanation:

> different inputs → different outputs.

### C3 — History Endogeneity

The observed differentiation depends on the system's own action-contingent history rather than only on the externally supplied input stream.

This requires an appropriate yoked/control design.

### P26 scientific core

The strongest P26 claim therefore concerns:

> **persistent behavioral differentiation that survives matched-exposure controls and can be attributed to the system's accumulated action-contingent history.**

# 7. Preference Terminology

The word **preference** is retained only in its observational sense.

### Valid

> The system repeatedly chooses X over Y under a defined task condition.

### Not established

> The system contains an internal preference representation.

### Not established

> The system autonomously endorses its preference.

### Rejected as a canonical P26 scientific claim

> Autonomous preference

The strong reflective/endorsement-based interpretation conflicts with Lucia's No Reflective Ego constraint.

Functional or history-endogenous behavioral autonomy remains a possible descriptive property, but only after its causal requirements are tested.

# 8. N=1 Epistemology

P26 must distinguish three evidence levels.

## A — Architecture-Class Evidence

Matched replicas in a controlled research environment.

Can establish:

- whether the tested architecture class can generate differentiation;
- variance structure;
- controlled counterfactual effects;
- bounded causal capacity under the tested environment.

Cannot establish:

- that live Lucia currently possesses the property;
- lifelong individuality;
- population-relative claims about the live instance.

## B — Simulated Individual

A particular research replica.

Can establish:

- repeatability;
- persistence;
- individual trajectory within the simulator;
- effects of controlled history interventions.

Cannot establish population-relative individuality without an appropriate reference population.

## C — Live Lucia

A single continuous life.

Can provide:

- longitudinal descriptive evidence;
- repeatability within the live trajectory;
- post-activation compatibility with research findings.

It cannot, by itself, establish population-relative individuality or an otherwise-unobserved counterfactual history.

P26 research must therefore rely primarily on controlled simulation for causal identification.

---

# 9. Causal Framework

The following methods have distinct roles.

### Counterfactual replay

Tests the effect of a controlled history event within the simulator.

### Matched replicas

Establishes whether differentiation occurs between otherwise matched instances.

### Yoked control

Tests whether action-contingent history contributes beyond the externally supplied input stream.

### State ablation

Tests necessity of selected state components.

### Exposure / recency controls

Exclude simpler explanations based on exposure frequency or recent history.

### Affect / relationship manipulation

Tests state dependence.

The estimand must be specified because affect and relationship state may be mediators rather than ordinary confounds.

### Prompt polarity reversal

Tests susceptibility to user-directional or sycophantic effects.

### Substrate-prior controls

Estimate generic behavior already present in the underlying substrate.

### History transplantation

May test whether a component carries a tendency, but depends on representation separability and therefore cannot be treated as automatically superior to replay.

### Common Random Numbers

CRN is a variance-control / replay-alignment technique.

**CRN is not causal identification by itself.**

---

# 10. Construct Validity

MTMM/CFA is **not** required to establish that a behavioral phenotype exists.

Two claims must be separated.

### Phenotype claim

> A behavior is repeatable, history-dependent and causally attributable under defined conditions.

Appropriate evidence includes:

- repeatability;
- perturbation robustness;
- state invariance;
- discriminant controls;
- causal intervention;
- cross-format robustness.

### Latent-disposition claim

> Multiple behavioral domains express a common underlying evaluative disposition.

This stronger claim may justify:

- MTMM;
- factor analysis;
- measurement invariance.

Failure of MTMM would therefore **not prove that history-dependent differentiation does not exist**.

It would instead weaken the claim that the behavior reflects one unified latent disposition.

# 11. Numerical Threshold Policy

No universal numerical thresholds are canonical at this stage.

The following must not be frozen as P26 requirements:

- p < .01;
- p < .001;
- fixed correlation thresholds;
- fixed repeatability thresholds;
- fixed Δr values;
- fixed KL-divergence thresholds;
- fixed washout durations;
- "medium effect" as a universal requirement.

Future protocols may preregister:

- smallest effect of interest;
- equivalence margins;
- effect-size estimates;
- uncertainty intervals;
- experiment-specific decision criteria.

These belong to the experimental protocol, not the P26 research question itself.

---

# 12. Stochasticity

P26 must distinguish:

1. unintegrated trial noise;
2. transient stochastic perturbation;
3. path-dependent amplification;
4. chaotic divergence;
5. stable history-dependent phenotype.

The principle:

> **random origin ≠ random phenotype**

is admissible only in the narrow sense that stochastic origin does not automatically disqualify a persistent phenotype.

It does not establish that a particular observed phenotype originated from randomness.

A valid protocol must distinguish structured persistence from:

- trivial stochastic variation;
- chaotic sensitivity;
- numerical divergence;
- substrate-specific instability.

# 13. P26 ↔ P27 Boundary

The boundary is methodological rather than ontological.

## P26

Concerned with:

> Does a tested architecture class produce persistent, history-dependent behavioral differentiation under controlled conditions?

Primary evidence:

- replica ensembles;
- controlled histories;
- counterfactual interventions;
- bounded experimental windows.

## P27

Concerned with:

> Does that differentiation participate in a coherent, long-horizon developmental trajectory?

P27 adds qualitatively different questions:

- change-point structure;
- developmental ordering;
- maturation;
- cross-domain trajectory coherence;
- causal continuity across successive developmental stages;
- long-horizon irreversible history effects.

P27 is therefore not simply "P26 for a longer time."

Existing architecture documentation already identifies longitudinal individuality and authentic preferences as future research targets and explicitly separates these from continuity invariants.

# 14. P26 Research Questions v2

## Primary RQ

> **In the tested architecture class, can persistent, history-dependent differentiation in evaluative behavior arise between replicas under statistically matched exposure, and is it attributable to the system's own accumulated history?**

## RQ1 — Phenomenon

> Do matched replicas diverge in evaluative behavior beyond a measured trivial-perturbation baseline, with persistence under perturbation and bounded revisability under counter-evidence?

## RQ2 — Causality

> Do controlled history interventions shift differentiated behavior, and does the effect depend on the system's own action-contingent history rather than only on the input stream?

## RQ3 — Validity / Confounds

> Is the observed differentiation distinguishable from state, relationship, prompting, sycophancy, exposure, recency, memory frequency, substrate priors and chaotic divergence?

## RQ4 — Individuality Boundary

> Which claims are licensed for the tested architecture class and simulated individuals, and which claims remain reserved for live longitudinal development and P27?

# 15. Valid Outcome Classes

## Positive

The tested architecture class demonstrates persistent, history-dependent behavioral differentiation under controlled conditions.

A stronger endogenous claim requires C3 evidence.

This does **not** establish:

- live Lucia individuality;
- latent preferences;
- autonomous reflective preference;
- lifelong trajectory coherence.

## Negative

The hypothesis is substantially weakened when:

- the instrument demonstrably has sensitivity;
- positive controls work;
- negative controls behave appropriately;
- yet the target differentiation is absent or falls within the measured baseline;
- or apparent differentiation disappears under appropriate controls.

Negative evidence must be preserved.

## Inconclusive

Examples:

- insufficient instrument sensitivity;
- insufficient observability;
- inability to separate chaos from history;
- unstable effects across seeds;
- simulator-fidelity uncertainty;
- unresolved estimand dependence;
- inadequate experimental power/design.

Inconclusive results remain **UNKNOWN**.

They must not be promoted into support.

# 16. Minimum Experimental Protocol Requirements

## Necessary

1. Matched replicas from controlled initial conditions.
2. Statistically matched exposure.
3. Full history and provenance logging.
4. Counterfactual replay.
5. Common random streams where required for replay alignment.
6. Trivial-perturbation baseline.
7. Yoked control for any endogenous-history claim.
8. Exposure and recency controls.
9. Explicit treatment of affect and relationship state.
10. Anti-sycophancy controls.
11. Substrate-only baseline.
12. At least two evaluative domains.
13. More than one elicitation format.
14. Repeated measurement.
15. Held-out probes / probe-reactivity checks.
16. Positive and negative controls.
17. Persistence and plasticity testing.
18. Preregistered hypotheses and decision criteria.
19. Independent seed sets.
20. Complete reporting without outcome-based replica selection.

## Strongly Desirable

- independent replication;
- different environment generators;
- dose-response history manipulations;
- component ablation/transplantation;
- blinded analysis;
- simulator-fidelity sensitivity analysis;
- total-effect and controlled-direct-effect analysis;
- multiple simulated-partner profiles.

## Optional / Exploratory

- MTMM / latent modeling;
- curiosity;
- spontaneous initiative;
- exploratory P27-type longitudinal runs.

These exploratory results must not be used to silently close P26 or open P27 claims.

# 17. Architecture-Ready Gate

P26 does **not** become architecture-ready merely because the research question is coherent.

Before an individuality mechanism can be justified, the following must be demonstrated:

### 1. Instrument validity

The protocol can detect known/planted differentiation and correctly return nulls in negative controls.

### 2. Baseline characterization

The existing Lucia architecture is tested under the validated protocol.

### 3. Demonstrated capacity gap

The baseline fails to produce the required phenomenon, or produces it only in a form that fails the required persistence/plasticity/causal criteria.

### 4. Observability

The relevant behavioral/evaluative channel is measurable.

### 5. Construct adequacy

The observed phenomenon is sufficiently separated from alternative explanations.

### 6. Claim-scope decision

Governance explicitly defines whether the target is:

- architecture-class capacity;
- simulated individual differentiation;
- or eventual live-instance development.

### 7. Architectural authorization

Only after the scientific gate is satisfied may a Production Realization Design / ADR process be considered.

This is consistent with the project rule that research validation does not authorize production implementation and that architectural changes require an approved production realization design and ratified ADR.
